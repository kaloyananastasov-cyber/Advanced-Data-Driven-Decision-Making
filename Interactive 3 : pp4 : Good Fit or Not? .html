First repair a badly fitting CFA model, as in the lecture example: apply changes and watch the fit measures react live. Then test whether two constructs are really distinct by moving their correlation.

1Repair the model slides 54 to 60 and 67 to 71

Five items measure loyalty in a one-factor CFA: two repurchase items (RI1, RI2) and three willingness-to-pay items (WTP1 to WTP3). Goal: reach a good fit (χ²/df below 5, RMSEA up to 0.05, CFI above 0.95) with changes you can justify. Click a change to apply it, click again to undo it.

Numbers on the right are modification indices: the expected drop in χ² if you make that change (slide 54).

2Discriminant validity lab slides 72 to 78

The standardized solution from slide 73. The AVE of each construct is the average of its squared loadings: η1 = 0.59, η2 = 0.64. Move the correlation between the constructs and watch the Fornell-Larcker test.

Challenge: find the highest correlation at which the two constructs still pass the Fornell-Larcker test, then lock it in.

3Your results

What to take away

  • Global fit: χ²/df below 5, RMSEA up to 0.05 (0.08 acceptable), CFI above 0.95. Judge them together.
  • Modification indices show where the model is wrong. Only make changes that theory can justify.
  • Freeing parameters until the fit is perfect capitalises on chance: the model will not hold in new data.
  • Discriminant validity: each AVE must exceed the squared correlation between the constructs (Fornell-Larcker).
