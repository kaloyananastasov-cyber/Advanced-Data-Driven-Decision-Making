What is rotation?

After extraction, think of the two factors as the axes of a chart. Each variable is an arrow, and its loadings are its coordinates on the two axes.

The first solution puts the axes where they explain the most variance. Often each variable then loads on both factors, so neither factor is easy to name.

Rotation turns the axes around the centre, like turning a map, until each arrow lies close to one axis. The arrows themselves do not move, so no information is lost: only the loadings change.

How to do it

  1. Move the slider below the chart and watch the red axes F1 and F2 turn.
  2. In the table, dark cells are loadings above 0.7. Aim for one dark cell in every row. Ignore the minus sign: −0.90 is as strong as 0.90.
  3. The red "simple structure" bar fills up as you get closer.
  4. When the bar is (almost) full, click Lock in my rotation.
  5. Answer the two questions below the chart.

Simple structure

2Use the rotated loadings to name the factors slides 56 and 57

Now the real study from slide 56: car owners answered nine questions about their car and its brand. Principal axis factoring found two factors, rotated with Varimax. To give each factor a name, you first need to know which items clearly belong to one factor. Only those items can tell you what the factor means.

The three rules from the lecture:

Keep and interpretLoads about 0.7 or more on one factor (slightly below is fine) and below 0.3 on all others. Use it to name that factor.
Don’t use to interpretLoads on one factor but also above 0.3 on another (a cross-loading). It belongs to two factors, so it cannot tell you what one factor means.
Consider deletingNo clear loading on any factor and a low communality: the factors explain little of this item.

Step A. For each item: find its largest loading, look at its other loadings, then choose the rule that applies.

Step B. Look at the items you kept for each factor and what they have in common.

What to take away

  • Rotation turns the axes. Loadings and the eigenvalue of each factor change; communalities and the total explained variance do not.
  • The goal is simple structure: each variable loads high on one factor and close to zero on the others. Varimax finds this angle for you.
  • Interpret a factor with items that load above about 0.7 on it and below 0.3 elsewhere.
  • Do not use items with a cross-loading above 0.3 to name a factor, and consider deleting items that load on no factor.
