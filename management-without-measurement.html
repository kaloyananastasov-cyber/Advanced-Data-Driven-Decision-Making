Management Without Measurement

Lecture 2 · Measurement and the Balanced Scorecard · slides 12 and 29
Your task:
You manage a coffee kiosk. Every week you choose a price and a promotion budget by clicking on the board. Somewhere on the board is the combination that makes the most profit. You have 12 weeks, so you click 12 times. For the first 6 weeks nobody measures your results, so you can only guess. From week 7 the accounting system tells you the profit of each week, and you can use it to find better decisions step by step.
Idea to discover: "Management without measurement is like a blind throw of a dart: some darts might hit the target, but without feedback, repetition of the success is pure random" (slide 12).
How to play: click on the board 12 times. One click = one week. The position of your click is your decision: left/right sets the price, up/down the promotion budget.

Guessing versus measuring

Discuss: In the guessing weeks, did you have any good week? Could you repeat it? What would happen if the measurement were wrong, for example an estimate from an AI tool that was never checked against real sales?
