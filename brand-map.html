The Brand Map Café

Application in marketing · Image analysis and brand positioning
Your task:
Café Lumen, a Geneva coffee chain, is losing guests and does not know why. 400 coffee drinkers rated Café Lumen and six competitors on eight image statements from 1 (disagree) to 7 (agree). Find out where guests see Café Lumen, and where it should go.
Idea to discover: a brand’s image is a point in the customer’s mind. Factor analysis turns many ratings into a map, and the map shows where the market is crowded and where it is empty.

2Eight statements, two hidden dimensions

Factor analysis on the 400 guests’ ratings finds that the eight statements are driven by two factors. The bars show the loadings: how strongly each statement moves with each factor. Bars to the left are negative.

Factor 1: Innovative ↔ Traditional

Factor 2: Luxury ↔ Value

Every brand now gets two factor scores instead of eight averages. Two numbers fit on a map.

What you just did is image analysis

You measured a brand’s image with many statements, reduced them to a few dimensions, placed the brands on a perceptual map, and used the customers’ ideal points to find a gap in the market.

Going further: a third dimension

Factor analysis can find more than two dimensions. Here a third factor, Cosy ↔ Grab-and-go (the atmosphere factor from the café exercise), adds depth. Drag the cube to turn it.

Look at Café Lumen and Casa Moka. On the 2D map they sit almost on top of each other. In 3D they are far apart: Café Lumen is cosy, Casa Moka is grab-and-go. A third dimension can reveal a difference, and a way to stand out, that two dimensions hide.
